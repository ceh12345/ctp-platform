## Problem Statement
How should a solo developer market CTP (a manufacturing scheduling/optimization
platform)? Initial instinct: target Genius ERP clients as a natural base because
a Genius adapter exists. Current state: one beta client (Stafford), no current
paying customers. Prior incarnation drew real interest.

## Demand Evidence
- **Prior version was bought** by a semiconductor manufacturer.
- **SysPro and QAD (ERP vendors)** ran formal buy-vs-build evaluations on embedding
  CTP and valued it at ~$2M.
- Those deals **stalled** — both vendors were going through ownership changes;
  attention moved elsewhere. Value was never the objection; timing on their side was.
- Critical nuance the founder surfaced: "Last go-round we had **zero end-customers**,
  but the **ERP companies saw the value**." The historical demand lived at the
  ERP-vendor layer, not the end-customer layer.
- Reachability of the old SysPro/QAD/semiconductor contacts post-M&A is unconfirmed;
  treat as cold until proven otherwise.

## Status Quo
Stafford (the beta) is a high-mix / low-volume (HMLV) make-to-order shop. The
scheduling pain is acute because every job differs, routings change, due dates
collide, and a slip triggers late-delivery penalties. Shops like this currently
cope with spreadsheets, tribal knowledge, and ERP modules that don't handle HMLV well.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge
- **Market = operating model, not ERP.** The buyer is an HMLV make-to-order
  manufacturer (Stafford's archetype). "Runs Genius" is a *filter* that lowers
  landing/integration cost — not the reason they buy. The Genius adapter is a
  landing-cost discount, not a market definition.
- **Wedge:** sell one quantified outcome (late-delivery penalty avoidance / on-time
  delivery lift) to shops that look exactly like Stafford.

## Constraints
- **Solo developer.** No sales team, no support org. This eliminates the
  enterprise OEM/embed motion and any broad "market to the world" push as a
  *starting* move.
- Marketing budget is effectively zero; the only affordable marketing is a hard
  outcome number from a reference customer.

## Premises (all agreed)
1. As a solo dev, the ERP-vendor/embed motion and broad marketing are off the table
   as the starting move. Start with one narrow niche wedge.
2. The market is defined by operating model (HMLV make-to-order), not by ERP. Genius
   is a cheap-landing filter, not the buying reason.
3. "What's next after Stafford" is premature. The next move is turning Stafford into
   a quantified, referenceable, ideally paying win. That number is the only
   affordable marketing.

## Key Insight (EUREKA)
The founder's money/validation came from a **different customer than the one the plan
targets**. SysPro/QAD valued CTP as an *embedded* module (OEM / sell-to-vendor). The
adapter plan targets *end-clients who run Genius* (sell-to-end-customer). These are two
different businesses with different buyers, cycles, and check sizes. The $2M evidence
does NOT transfer to the end-client motion. Stafford is the first real attempt to
generate proof at the end-customer layer the vendors never required.

## Approaches Considered
### Approach A: Clone hunt (do things that don't scale)  — RECOMMENDED
Get the Stafford number, then hand-sell 3-5 shops identical to Stafford (HMLV MTO,
similar size, ideally Genius) 1:1 on the case study. Founder-led outbound, no infra.
Effort S-M, Risk Med. Reuses Stafford case study + Genius adapter. AI can build the
prospect list, draft outbound, and build an ROI/penalty calculator; selling stays human.

### Approach B: Genius as channel
Use the Stafford win to make Genius (the company) a referral/resale partner for their
HMLV clients. One yes unlocks a qualified list. Effort M-L, Risk Med-High. Slow,
partner-dependent, only credible after proof exists. Phase-2 move, not a start.

### Approach C: Niche authority (inbound)
Become the findable specialist for "scheduling for HMLV make-to-order" via a penalty
calculator, APS-fails-for-high-mix teardown, anonymized Stafford benchmarks. Effort M,
compounding. Risk Med. Pairs with A; weak alone.

## Recommended Approach
**A as primary, with an optional thin slice of C in parallel.** B and C both run on
fuel the founder doesn't have yet (B needs proof to interest Genius; C needs traffic
and time). A produces the next 1-3 paying near-clones, which are exactly what make B
credible and C substantive. B is the phase-2 prize once ~3 logos exist.

## Open Questions / Decision Pending
- **GTM motion NOT yet committed.** Founder is deliberating A vs B vs C ("let me think
  it over"). This doc records the recommendation, not a final choice.
- Will Stafford pay, and what is the clean outcome metric (which penalty/KPI, baseline
  vs after)? Pricing for the clone-hunt deals is undefined.
- Are any prior SysPro/QAD/semiconductor contacts still reachable post-M&A?

## Success Criteria
- A single defensible Stafford number (e.g., "$X late-penalty avoidance" or
  "on-time X% → Y%") that can headline a one-page case study.
- Within ~90 days of that number: 3-5 qualified clone prospects contacted; ≥1 second
  paying/committed reference.

## The Assignment
Define and instrument the **one number** you will extract from Stafford before you
chase anyone else. Pick the single metric Stafford's owner already cares about (the
late-delivery penalty figure is the strongest candidate per their stated value driver),
establish the baseline, and decide how CTP's result against it will be measured. You
can't market a number you haven't defined.
